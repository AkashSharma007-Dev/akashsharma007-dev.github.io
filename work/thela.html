Thela checkout

A redesign of the cart and payment steps in a grocery delivery app, so the price in the cart is the price you pay.

Project
Concept redesign, mobile app
Platform
iPhone and Android
My part
Audit, flow, wireframes, visual design, prototype
Screens
2 before, 3 after
Open the prototype

The problem

In the old flow the cart shows ₹486. One tap later the payment screen asks for ₹545. Four fees worth ₹59 appear for the first time just before paying, when it feels too late to change anything.

UPI, the way most people in India pay for everyday things online, sits under "More payment options", below cards, wallets and net banking.

Thela is fictional, and so are both "before" screens. I designed them to show a pattern that is common in delivery apps, so the redesign has something concrete to fix.

What I found

The numbers match the red markers on the before screens.

  1. The coupon field is the first thing in the cart. It sends people away to hunt for codes.
  2. A row of suggested snacks pushes the actual cart below the fold.
  3. The cart total counts items only, with no hint that fees will be added.
  4. Delivery, handling, late-night and platform fees, ₹59 in all, appear first on the payment screen.
  5. UPI is hidden under "More payment options".

Who it's for

Someone topping up daily groceries, often late in the evening, with one hand, who wants the order fast and without surprises. This is my assumption for the concept, not a finding from research.

The flow

  1. Cart with every fee
  2. Choose payment
  3. Pay by UPI
  4. Order placed

The old flow had the same number of steps. The change is what each step tells you, and when.

Wireframes

The new screens in grey, to get the order of information right: when it arrives, what is in it, what happens if something is missing, and what it all costs.

Design decisions

  1. Every fee in the cart, with its reason

    The fees did not go away. They moved to the cart, each with a short reason: the late-night fee says it applies between 11 pm and 6 am. The total under the button is now the amount that will be charged.

  2. An honest nudge instead of a snack row

    The upsell row is gone. In its place, a bar shows how close the order is to free delivery: ₹13 more. People add something they need instead of something placed in front of them.

  3. Ask about missing items once, before paying

    A three-way choice in the cart: send a similar item, remove it, or call me. No surprise substitutions, and no call from the store at 11 pm.

  4. UPI first, and the same total

    The payment screen leads with UPI apps on the phone. Its title repeats the cart total, and a line under the options says no new charges were added. Cash on delivery reminds people to keep exact change.

  5. A confirmation that answers the next question

    After paying, the screen shows the arrival time, who is bringing the order, and the choices made in the cart, including what happens if something is out of stock.

What I would test next

  1. Run fees-in-cart against fees-at-payment and compare completed orders and complaints about charges, not only how many people tap Pay.
  2. Check whether the free-delivery bar leads people to add useful items, or only adds pressure.
  3. Try "Send similar" and "Call me" as the default and compare refunds and complaints.
  4. Test UPI first with people who mostly pay cash on delivery, especially outside big cities.

Thela is a concept project. The app, its stores, riders and all figures are fictional.
